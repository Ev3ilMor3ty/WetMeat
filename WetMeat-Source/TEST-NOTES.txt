Recorded head-to-head study
---------------------------
420 completed training games against Stockfish 19 at 100 ms per move:
55 wins, 318 draws, 47 losses for the Linux AVX-512 WetMeat binary.
Both engines: one thread, 128 MB hash, full Skill Level 20, no Elo limit,
no ponder or tablebases, paired colours and rotating matched CPU assignments.

The first 100 fully audited games scored 14 wins, 72 draws and 14 losses.
Twenty completed batch exports cover 400 fully audited games:

                  Moves checked    Mistakes    Blunders    Shallower judge depth
WetMeat           25379 / 25379     169         36          1523
Stockfish 19      25355 / 25355     163         35          1040

Mistakes are 50–199 cp loss; blunders are at least 200 cp, under a finite
Stockfish-family judge with larger node budgets and common completed-depth
comparisons. Opening-book moves are excluded. Moves and opening pairs are
correlated. Won/lost positions can produce large cp changes without a meaningful
change in practical result. These are finite sample classifications.

The remaining 20 completed games have partial audits. The planned 1000-game
study is incomplete, so there is no complete 420-game error rate or final
1000-game strength conclusion. The sample does not establish a lower error
rate, general superiority, a calibrated Elo or perfect play.

The head-to-head scores were measured with Linux AVX-512 only. Other builds
use the same source and model but retain their own identities and do not
inherit that binary's match measurements. No experimental candidate was adopted.

Earlier studies, prototypes and experiments remain separate. Research source
is included for reference, not as a stronger replacement for the stable engine.
